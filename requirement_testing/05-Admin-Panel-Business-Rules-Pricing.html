Zinto Platform — Manual Requirement Testing Guide
This document is a step-by-step checklist for confirming that the money-related rules of the Zinto platform work as intended — how commission is charged, how prices and fees are built up, how packaging charges are calculated, and how order timing rules behave. It is meant for a non-technical person: log in to the Zinto Admin Panel (and, for a few checks, the customer app and seller panel) and follow each numbered test exactly as written, tapping the same buttons and menus described here.

How to use this guide

Each test below has a number and a small reference tag pointing back to the original requirement list. Follow the numbered steps in order, using the real screen and button names given. After finishing the steps, compare what you see on screen with the ✅ Expected Result line — if it matches, you can tick that test off as working. A few items describe a rule that runs automatically in the background rather than something you click through; those are marked "How this is confirmed" instead of steps.

1.1 Product Vision

These are big-picture checks that confirm Zinto behaves like a full multi-seller, multi-city, hyperlocal delivery platform — not deep settings, just simple things you can see and confirm while browsing the live app.

Test 1 — More than one seller sells on the platform Ref: Req #1

  1. Open the Zinto customer app or website.
  2. Browse the home screen and open two or three different products, or open two different store pages.
  3. Look for the seller/store name shown on each product card or store page.
✅ Expected Result: You can see products belonging to more than one different seller/store name — confirming this is a shared marketplace, not a single shop.

Test 2 — The app works in more than one city Ref: Req #2

  1. Open the Zinto customer app and tap the location/city selector at the top of the screen.
  2. Search for or switch to a different city than the one currently shown.
  3. Confirm the store list and products on the home screen refresh for the new city.
✅ Expected Result: Changing the city changes which stores and products are shown, confirming the app supports more than one city/location.

Test 3 — Orders are hyperlocal (fast, nearby delivery) Ref: Req #3

  1. Open the customer app, set your current delivery address.
  2. Open a nearby store and look at the delivery time estimate shown on the store page or at checkout.
✅ Expected Result: The app shows nearby stores only (not stores from far-away cities) and gives a short, local delivery time estimate (e.g. minutes, not days).

Test 4 — The catalog can hold a very large number of products Ref: Req #4

  1. Log in to the Admin Panel and open Products from the left menu.
  2. Check the product list/table — note the total product count and page numbers at the bottom.
  3. Try searching for a product by name and confirm results appear instantly.
✅ Expected Result: The Products screen shows a large product count with working pagination and search, without the page freezing or timing out.

Test 5 — The platform can handle many users at once Ref: Req #5

How this is confirmed: This is about the app staying fast and stable when many customers use it at the same time. It cannot be checked by clicking a single screen — it is confirmed by the technical team through load testing and by observing that the live app stays responsive during busy hours.

Test 6 — Products are organised into categories and subcategories Ref: Req #6

  1. In the Admin Panel, open the Categories menu and check its three levels: Header Categories, Main Categories, and Sub-Categories.
  2. Confirm each level lists categories that clearly nest under one another (e.g. a Header Category containing several Main Categories).
  3. Now open the customer app and browse the home screen category bar — confirm you can drill from a broad category into narrower subcategories.
✅ Expected Result: Both the Admin Panel and the customer app show a clear category → subcategory structure that products are organised under.

Test 7 — Sellers can join under a subscription plan or a commission plan Ref: Req #7

  1. In the Admin Panel, open Sellers → Subscriptions.
  2. Confirm you can see subscription plans configured for sellers (e.g. plan names, prices, limits).
  3. Open Fees & Charges and locate the "Free-Tier Product Limit" section, which describes commission-based sellers vs. subscription-tier sellers.
✅ Expected Result: The platform clearly supports two business models for sellers — a subscription plan, and a commission-based (pay-per-sale) plan.

Test 8 — Orders move through a full set of stages Ref: Req #8

  1. In the Admin Panel, open the Orders menu and look at its sub-items: All Orders, New Orders, Being Prepared, On the Way, Delivered, Cancelled, Returned, Return Requests, Refund Ledger.
  2. Place a test order as a customer and watch it move through these stages by checking the Admin Orders list periodically.
✅ Expected Result: An order visibly progresses through several real stages (placed → prepared → on the way → delivered), and cancelled/returned orders have their own dedicated views.

Test 9 — Prices are built up from several different charges Ref: Req #9

  1. As a customer, add a product to your cart and proceed to checkout.
  2. Look at the price summary/bill shown before payment.
✅ Expected Result: The checkout bill shows more than just the product price — separate lines for things like delivery fee, handling/platform fee, and taxes, which can each change from order to order.

Test 10 — You can search for products and discover stores Ref: Req #10

  1. On the customer app home screen, tap the search bar and search for a product by name.
  2. Separately, browse the "stores near you" or store-listing section of the app.
✅ Expected Result: Searching returns matching products, and you can browse and open different nearby stores from a store-discovery section of the app.

Test 11 — Orders can be scheduled for a future time Ref: Req #11

  1. As a customer, add items to your cart and go to checkout.
  2. Look for a delivery scheduling option (e.g. choosing a future date/time slot instead of "as soon as possible").
  3. Select a future slot and place the order.
✅ Expected Result: You can choose to schedule delivery for a later date/time instead of immediate delivery, and the order is placed successfully for that slot.

Test 12 — Refunds, returns, cancellations and replacements are supported Ref: Req #12

  1. Open a delivered order in the customer app's Order Details screen.
  2. Check for a "Return" or "Request Return/Replacement" option, and for a "Cancel Order" option on an order that hasn't been delivered yet.
  3. In the Admin Panel, open Orders → Return Requests and Orders → Refund Ledger to confirm returns and refunds are tracked centrally.
✅ Expected Result: Customers can cancel, return, or request a replacement from within their order, and the Admin Panel shows a matching return request and refund record.

Test 13 — Admin can configure how the platform behaves, without needing a developer Ref: Req #13

  1. In the Admin Panel, open Fees & Charges and Settings.
  2. Change a simple value (for example, the "Free Delivery Minimum" amount) and save it.
  3. Confirm the change is accepted with a success message.
✅ Expected Result: Admin staff can change platform behaviour (fees, limits, charges, etc.) directly from these settings screens, with no code changes needed.

Test 14 — Commission can be set up and changed by admin, at multiple levels Ref: Req #96

  1. In the Admin Panel, open Categories → Header Categories and edit a category. Find the "Apply commission at this level" checkbox and the "Admin Commission (%)" field.
  2. Do the same on Categories → Main Categories and Categories → Sub-Categories.
  3. Open Products, edit a product, and find the "Apply product commission" checkbox with a Commission Type (Percentage/Fixed) and value field.
✅ Expected Result: Commission percentages/amounts can be turned on and edited at the category, subcategory, and product level, all from the Admin Panel, and the values save successfully.

Test 15 — Commission can be set per city Ref: Req #97

  1. In the Admin Panel, open City Commissions from the left menu.
  2. Enter a city key (e.g. "indore") and city name, choose a Commission Type (Percentage or Fixed), enter a value, tick "Apply commission for this city", and click "Save City Commission".
  3. Confirm the new city appears in the "Configured Cities" table below with the commission you entered.
✅ Expected Result: A commission rate can be saved against a specific city and shows up correctly in the city commission list.

Test 16 — Commission can be set per individual shop/seller Ref: Req #98

  1. In the Admin Panel, open Sellers → Active Sellers and open a seller's profile/details page.
  2. Look for any field on that seller's page letting you set a commission rate that applies only to that one shop.
✅ Expected Result: There should be a way to give one specific shop its own commission rate, separate from the category or city rate it would otherwise fall back to.

Test 17 — Commission can be set per category Ref: Req #99

  1. In the Admin Panel, open Categories → Header Categories.
  2. Edit any category, tick "Apply commission at this level", enter a value in "Admin Commission (%)", and save.
  3. Confirm the category's row in the table now shows that commission percentage.
✅ Expected Result: The category list shows the commission percentage you just set for that category.

Test 18 — Commission can be set per sub-category Ref: Req #100

  1. In the Admin Panel, open Categories → Sub-Categories.
  2. Edit a subcategory, tick "Apply commission at this level", enter a value, and save.
  3. Note the on-screen hint: "Deepest level wins. If this is applied, it overrides Header and Level 2 commission."
✅ Expected Result: The subcategory's commission saves correctly and, when set, takes priority over its parent Main Category and Header Category commission.

Test 19 — Commission can be set per individual product Ref: Req #101

  1. In the Admin Panel, open Products, and edit any product.
  2. Tick "Apply product commission", choose a Commission Type, and enter a value.
  3. Save and confirm the product's own commission is stored (check the on-screen note: "When enabled, this overrides category commission at checkout.").
✅ Expected Result: A single product can be given its own commission rate that overrides whatever its category would normally apply.

Test 20 — Commission can be set per product variant Ref: Req #102

  1. In the Admin Panel, open Products and edit a product that has variants (e.g. different sizes/weights).
  2. Scroll to the variants list and look for a "Variant-specific commission" checkbox and percentage field next to an individual variant.
  3. Turn it on, enter a value, and save.
✅ Expected Result: One specific variant of a product can have its own commission percentage that overrides both the product-level and category-level commission for that variant only.

Test 21 — Commission can be set per add-on product Ref: Req #103

  1. In the Admin Panel, open Products and open/create a product marked as an add-on type product.
  2. Check whether the same "Apply product commission" section (Commission Type + value) is available and works for this add-on item, the same way it does for a regular product.
✅ Expected Result: An add-on product can be given its own commission rate, separate from the main product it is attached to.

Test 22 — Commission can be set at the whole-shop level Ref: Req #104

  1. In the Admin Panel, open a seller's profile under Sellers → Active Sellers.
  2. Look for a single "shop-level commission" setting that would apply to every product from that shop, unless a more specific (product/category) commission overrides it.
✅ Expected Result: A shop-wide commission rate can be configured for one seller's entire store.

Test 23 — Multiple separate commission "layers" exist (user, shop, category, platform) Ref: Req #105

  1. Review the commission fields you have already seen across City Commissions, Categories, Products, and any seller-level commission field.
  2. Confirm each of these is a genuinely separate configurable component rather than one single global number.
✅ Expected Result: Commission is not just one flat platform-wide number — it is made up of distinct, separately configurable pieces (by city, by category, by shop, by product) that combine to decide the final commission on a sale.

Test 24 — Sellers enter their own selling price for a product Ref: Req #106

  1. Log in to the Seller Panel and open the screen for adding/editing a product.
  2. Enter a price in the product's price field.
✅ Expected Result: The seller can type in their own base selling price for a product — this is the amount the seller expects to receive.

Test 25 — The platform adds commission, GST, and packing charges on top of the seller's price Ref: Req #107

  1. While adding a product in the Seller Panel, look at the live "customer will pay" price preview shown next to your entered price.
  2. Confirm the customer-facing price is higher than the price you typed.
✅ Expected Result: The price shown to the customer is automatically higher than the seller's entered price, because commission, GST, and packing charges have been added on top.

Test 26 — Cart/order-level extra charges exist (handling, platform, weather, odd-hours, delivery) Ref: Req #108

  1. In the Admin Panel, open Fees & Charges and review the sections: "Main Charges" (Platform/Handling Fee), "Apply Extra Charge (Customers Only)" (weather-style surcharge), "Odd-Hour Delivery Charge", "Weather Surcharge", and "Delivery Fee Settings".
  2. As a customer, place an order and check the checkout bill for matching charge lines.
✅ Expected Result: All of these charges can be independently turned on/off and configured from the Admin Panel, and the ones that are enabled appear correctly on the customer's checkout bill.

Test 27 — Only admin can control commission, not sellers Ref: Req #109

  1. Log in to the Seller Panel and open the "Add Product" screen.
  2. Check that there is no field allowing the seller to set or change their own commission rate — only a price field and a read-only "customer will pay" preview.
✅ Expected Result: Sellers can only set their product's selling price; they cannot see or edit commission rates — that control stays fully with the Admin Panel.

8. Pricing, Charges, Fee Components & Invoice System

These tests confirm the customer's final bill is broken down clearly, that admin can control each fee, and that proper GST invoices are available afterward.

Test 28 — The order bill shows a full breakup of every charge Ref: Req #110

  1. As a customer, place an order and open its Order Details screen afterward.
  2. Scroll to the price/bill section and check for separate lines such as: product subtotal, Delivery Fee, Handling Fee, Packaging Charge, any active surcharge (weather/odd-hour/other), and GST (shown as CGST + SGST, or IGST for inter-state orders).
✅ Expected Result: The order bill clearly itemises product cost, handling fee, GST, delivery fee, any weather/odd-hour charges, packaging charge, and platform-related charges as separate lines rather than one lump total.

Test 29 — Product/category-level charges cover commission, GST, and packaging Ref: Req #111

  1. In the Admin Panel, edit a category under Categories → Header Categories (or Main/Sub-Categories) and check it has fields for "Admin Commission (%)" and "GST Slab (%)".
  2. Open Products, edit a product, and check it has its own "Packaging Charge (₹)" field that can override the category/store default.
✅ Expected Result: Commission, GST, and packaging charge can each be configured at the product or category level, with a product-level value overriding the category default where set.

Test 30 — Cart/order-level charges cover handling, platform, delivery, weather and odd-hour fees Ref: Req #112

  1. In the Admin Panel, open Fees & Charges and confirm you can edit: Platform/Handling Fee, Delivery Fee Settings (fixed or distance-based), Odd-Hour Delivery Charge, and Weather Surcharge.
  2. Save a change to one of them and confirm it takes effect at checkout on the customer app.
✅ Expected Result: Each of these order-level charges is independently configurable by admin and correctly reflected on the customer's checkout bill.

Test 31 — Customers can get a proper GST invoice for their order Ref: Req #113

  1. As a customer, open a delivered order's Order Details screen.
  2. Tap the "Invoice" button (with a download icon) near the top of the order summary.
  3. Confirm a "Tax Invoice" preview opens, and tap "Download Official PDF" (shown once the order is delivered).
✅ Expected Result: A GST tax invoice is generated automatically for the order and can be viewed and downloaded as a PDF by the customer.

Test 32 — Sellers can get a GST invoice for their orders Ref: Req #114

  1. Log in to the Seller Panel and open the GST Invoices screen.
  2. Find a delivered order in the list and tap its "Invoice" action.
✅ Expected Result: The seller can view and download a GST invoice for each of their delivered orders once it has been generated.

Test 33 — Charges are configurable from the Admin Panel Ref: Req #115

  1. In the Admin Panel, open Fees & Charges.
  2. Change the amount in one of: "Charge Amount" under Odd-Hour Delivery Charge, "Charge Amount" under Weather Surcharge, "Base Delivery Charge", or "Platform/Handling Fee".
  3. Click "Save Changes" at the top of the screen and confirm a success message appears.
✅ Expected Result: Packaging, handling, delivery, weather-based, and odd-hour charges can all be changed directly from the Admin Panel without any code changes, and the new values are saved.

9. Order Timing Rules & Order Placement Restrictions

These tests confirm rules around what time of day orders can be placed, and how late-night ("odd-hour") deliveries are charged extra.

Test 34 — No new orders can be placed after 10 PM Ref: Req #116

  1. Open the customer app shortly after 10:00 PM (or ask someone to change the test device's clock to a time after 10:00 PM, if available).
  2. Add a product to your cart and try to complete checkout by placing the order.
✅ Expected Result: The app stops you from placing the order after 10:00 PM and shows a clear message explaining that ordering is closed for the day/until a certain time.

Test 35 — Odd-hour deliveries are supported and charged extra, controlled by admin Ref: Req #117

  1. In the Admin Panel, open Fees & Charges and find the "Odd-Hour Delivery Charge" section.
  2. Turn the toggle on, set a "Charge Amount (₹)", set "Window Start" and "Window End" times (for example 22:00 to 06:00), and save.
  3. As a customer, try placing an order while the current time falls inside that window and check the checkout bill.
✅ Expected Result: Orders placed inside the configured odd-hour window automatically include the odd-hour surcharge on the bill, and admin can freely change the charge amount and time window.

10. Packaging Charges & Packaging Logic

These tests confirm packaging charges are not a single flat number, but can vary sensibly depending on the type of product.

Test 36 — Packaging charges vary by category and how a product needs to be handled Ref: Req #118

  1. In the Admin Panel, open Categories → Header Categories (or Main/Sub-Categories) and edit two different categories — for example, a fragile/perishable category and a simple packaged-goods category.
  2. Give each category a different value in "Packing Fees (₹)".
  3. Open Products and edit one product, setting a "Packaging Charge (₹)" that is different again from its category's default.
  4. Place a test order containing products from these different categories and check the "Packaging Charge" line on the checkout bill.
✅ Expected Result: The packaging charge on the bill changes depending on the product/category ordered, and a product's own packaging charge overrides its category's default when one is set.

Test 37 — The system has a proper place to store packaging categories and packaging charge rules Ref: Req #119

  1. In the Admin Panel, open Categories → Header Categories, Main Categories, and Sub-Categories and confirm each one has its own "Packing Fees (₹)" field that saves independently.
  2. Open Products and confirm each product also has its own optional "Packaging Charge (₹)" override field.
✅ Expected Result: Packaging charges are properly organised by category level and by individual product, not hard-coded as one single number for the whole platform.
